THE CORE ASK

KYC & investment regulation for onboarding

This is the JD's explicit differentiator — "extensive knowledge in the wealth domain, with particular emphasis on KYC, investment regulations, and onboarding." Learn this section closely; it's where a domain-gap question will actually be tested.

Who regulates this
The SFC sets the suitability and product rules (Code of Conduct for Persons Licensed by or Registered with the SFC); the HKMA supervises banks as "Registered Institutions" applying those same SFC rules when they sell investment products; the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) oversees CRS/FATCA tax-residency reporting separately from either of those.
Suitability requirement
Code of Conduct paragraphs 5.2 and 5.5(a): any recommendation or solicitation to a client, or any transaction in a complex product, must be "suitable for the client in all the circumstances," based on information the bank has or reasonably should have about that client. This is the rule underneath almost everything else in this section.
"Complex product" definition
An investment product whose terms, features and risks are not reasonably likely to be understood by a retail investor because of its complex structure. DBS's own website explicitly labels its Equity-Linked and Currency-Linked Investments (structured notes), and some bonds and investment funds, as complex products requiring extra caution and explanation before sale.
The Risk Profile Questionnaire (RPQ)
Central to onboarding: captures investment objectives, investment experience & knowledge, financial situation and risk tolerance, feeding a client risk-tolerance classification that gates which products can be sold to that client. The SFC has specifically flagged as a supervisory finding that some firms lacked controls to detect abnormally frequent RPQ updates — a real signal that a client (or an over-eager RM) is re-answering the questionnaire just to unlock a product. Worth knowing this is a live audit point, not a hypothetical one.
Professional Investors & the "Sophisticated PI" streamline
An Individual Professional Investor generally needs a portfolio of at least HK$8 million. Since a joint HKMA/SFC circular of July 2023, intermediaries can apply a "Streamlined Approach" for Sophisticated PIs — clients assessed as having genuine investment sophistication and loss-absorption ability — letting them set aside a defined amount for investment without the full suitability assessment on each transaction. An intermediary can rely on information already gathered during onboarding/KYC to make that determination.
CRS & FATCA — the tax-residency piece
Legal basis: the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 3) Ordinance 2016, in force since 1 Jan 2017 (Automatic Exchange of Financial Account Information, AEOI). Every reporting financial institution must identify the tax residency of account holders and, for entities, their controlling persons — via one of three self-certification form types: Individual, Entity, or Controlling Person. FATCA (US persons) and CRS (all other reportable jurisdictions) are legally separate — a client who's already given FATCA information may still need to complete CRS separately. The Hong Kong TIN-equivalent is the HKID number for individuals and the Business Registration number for entities. Clients must give an updated self-certification within 30 days of any change to their tax residency.
FI reporting obligations (context, not something a BA files personally)
FIs must register with IRD's AEOI portal within 3 months of first holding a reportable account, and submit Financial Account Information Returns within 5 months of IRD's annual January notice; FATCA compliance is certified to the IRS roughly every 3 years. Useful context for why the onboarding system has to capture clean, structured tax-residency data at the point of account opening — bad data here becomes a downstream statutory-reporting problem.
Mandatory information typically collected at onboarding
Identity & address verification (HKID/passport, proof of address); tax residency self-certification (CRS/FATCA); investment objectives, investment horizon, investment experience & knowledge, financial situation/net worth (the RPQ inputs); source of wealth/funds (particularly for higher-risk or complex-product transactions, and for AML purposes); employment/occupation; PEP and sanctions screening; and, for US persons specifically, the relevant IRS form (W-9 for US persons, W-8BEN for non-US persons certifying foreign status).
The historical "why" — worth knowing for DBS specifically
Much of today's suitability/complex-product regime traces back to the 2008 Lehman Brothers collapse, when structured notes marketed to retail investors across Hong Kong — including DBS's own "Constellation Notes" and "Octave Notes" (alongside Lehman's "Minibonds") — were mis-sold to customers who didn't understand the credit-default-swap structure underneath. This is genuinely why the current RPQ/complex-product/suitability machinery exists, and it's a strong thing to reference if asked why this domain knowledge matters at all.
Two more onboarding-adjacent protections worth naming
A Pre-Investment Cooling-off Period (PICOP) exists for vulnerable or elderly customers on certain products, giving them a mandatory pause before a transaction completes. Sales of complex products to certain client segments also generally require audio recording of the sales process, and a Key Facts Statement (KFS) must be provided so the client sees a plain-language risk summary before investing — both worth knowing exist even if you don't cite the precise scope from memory.

FOR YOUR REFERENCE

Typical release management steps

A generic, industry-standard release lifecycle for a regulated financial platform — not DBS's actual internal process, which you don't have visibility into yet. Useful as a structure to reason from if asked to walk through how you'd manage a digiWealth release, and as a checklist once you're actually in the role.

Requirements & impact assessment

Capture the business/regulatory requirement (BRD/FRD), assess impact across upstream and downstream systems, and identify which teams/platforms have a dependency on this release.

Check whether the change touches RPQ logic, complex-product classification, or CRS/FATCA data capture — any of those usually pulls in Compliance and Legal from this step, not later.

Design & solution review

Technical design is reviewed against the requirement; architecture and integration touchpoints with other platforms are confirmed; a rollback/fallback approach is agreed before build starts.

Build / configuration

Development or configuration happens in sprints (if agile) against the agreed design, with the BA tracking scope against the original requirement and flagging drift early.

Test planning & environment sync

Test cases are written against requirements (including negative/edge cases); test environments are synced with production-like data and configuration so results are trustworthy; dependencies with other teams' release calendars are checked so integration environments don't conflict.

System Integration Testing (SIT)

Cross-system flows are tested end-to-end — for an onboarding journey, that means identity verification, RPQ scoring, product-eligibility gating, and CRS/FATCA data capture all working together, not just each piece in isolation.

User Acceptance Testing (UAT)

Business users (and, where relevant, Compliance) sign off that the built solution actually meets the original requirement — not just that it's technically bug-free. Defects triaged by severity; sign-off is a formal, documented gate, not an informal nod.

For anything touching suitability or complex-product classification, expect Compliance sign-off to be a hard gate here, not a courtesy review.

Regulatory / compliance sign-off

Where the change affects a regulated process — onboarding, suitability assessment, tax-residency capture — a formal compliance or legal sign-off is obtained and documented, separate from UAT sign-off.

Release readiness / go–no-go

A go/no-go checklist is reviewed with all dependent teams — open defects, outstanding risk items, rollback plan confirmed, comms to affected stakeholders prepared.

Deployment

Release goes into production, usually in a controlled window; for consumer-facing wealth platforms this is often phased (soft launch to a subset of users/branches before full rollout) rather than a single big-bang cutover.

Post-release monitoring (hypercare)

Elevated monitoring for an agreed period immediately after go-live — watching for production incidents, unexpected volumes, or edge cases the test environment didn't surface — with a fast escalation path back to the build team.

Closure & lessons learned

Formal closure against the original business case/requirement, PMO artefacts updated, and a retrospective capturing what to change for the next release — the same closure discipline you already apply to your own de-scoped projects.
